Before you trust an AI answer: a verification checklist.

A confident tone is not evidence. Before you use an AI-generated answer in an assignment, a work decision, or a personal project, take a few minutes to check what supports it and where it might fail.

Practical workflow · Approximately 5 minutes

Step 1: Separate the answer into checkable claims

Do not try to verify a long response as one block. Break it into individual claims: dates, names, definitions, numerical values, quotations, instructions, and cause-and-effect statements. Mark which claims are essential to the decision you need to make.

For a short answer, a simple list is enough. For a longer answer, make a table with the claim, the evidence needed, the source checked, and the result.

Step 2: Find the right kind of source

Choose evidence appropriate to the claim. For a course topic, start with your prescribed textbook, lecture material, or instructor guidance. For a product feature, check the manufacturer's current documentation. For a policy or deadline, use the official organization responsible for it. A search result snippet or another AI answer is not a substitute for reading the underlying source.

  • Prefer primary or official sources when they are available.
  • Check the publication or update date for changing information.
  • Open the source and confirm that it actually supports the claim.
  • Use more than one independent reliable source when the stakes justify it.

Step 3: Verify numbers, quotes, and references separately

Numerical answers should be recalculated when possible. Check units, assumptions, rounding, and the inputs used. For quotations, search the original source and verify the exact wording and context. For citations, make sure the cited page exists and supports the sentence attached to it.

Never treat a citation as proof just because it looks formal. AI systems can produce plausible-looking but inaccurate titles, links, page numbers, or quotations.

Step 4: Ask what is missing or overstated

Even a mostly correct response can be misleading if it leaves out a key condition. Look for absolute language such as “always,” “never,” or “guaranteed.” Check whether the answer explains assumptions, exceptions, uncertainty, and the limits of the evidence.

Try asking: “What assumptions does this answer rely on?”, “Which claims are not directly supported by the sources?”, and “What would change this conclusion?” These questions can reveal gaps, but their answers still need checking.

Step 5: Match the level of checking to the risk

A low-stakes brainstorming suggestion may need only a quick sanity check. A claim that affects health, legal rights, money, safety, or an important academic submission deserves much stronger verification with qualified or authoritative sources. Do not rely on an AI response as the sole basis for a high-stakes decision.

Risk levelReasonable next step
LowCheck for obvious errors and test whether the suggestion works.
ModerateVerify important claims against reliable sources and document corrections.
HighConsult authoritative sources or a qualified professional; do not rely on AI alone.
